# Martin’s value-first playbook

## Lead with value, not a feature inventory
The hierarchy is **customer outcome → why it matters → credible proof or demonstration → optional technical details**.

A feature is what the product has. A benefit is what that feature enables. An outcome is the change the customer actually wants. Never invent results or imply guaranteed savings.

## Editing process
1. Identify the audience, job-to-be-done and primary action.
2. Inventory features internally. Do not automatically show each as a card.
3. Cluster features by the customer problem they solve.
4. Choose the strongest defensible outcome for the hero.
5. Choose two or three distinct supporting benefits where appropriate.
6. Use a real demo, example or trustworthy evidence to support claims.
7. Keep the complete feature list discoverable when buyers need technical details.

## Examples (illustrative, verify against the product)
| Feature-heavy | Value-first |
|---|---|
| AI prioritization, labels, notifications | Know what to tackle next. |
| Dashboards, charts, reports | Spot what is working without digging. |
| Integrations with 12 tools | Keep working in the tools you already use. |
| Templates, triggers, automations | Spend less time on repetitive steps. |

## Questions for each section
- What meaningful visitor question does this answer?
- Which customer outcome does it clarify or prove?
- Is this new information or a repeated feature list?
- Can it be merged, shortened or moved without increasing uncertainty?
- Does it contain essential limitations or buying criteria that must stay?

## Don't do this
- Replace factual technical requirements with vague marketing promises.
- Invent benefits, ROI, speed gains, logos, customer quotes or proof.
- Hide pricing, eligibility, limitations, security or integration details.
- Enforce a rigid number of benefits for every audience and product.

Martin's verdict: “You built 27 features. Now tell people why they should care.”
