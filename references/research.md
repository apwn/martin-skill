# Research and evidence limits

Rules folded into the skill, and where they came from. Only concrete, non-duplicative points. No study here proves that shorter copy or a shorter hero raises conversion. That needs an experiment on the real page.

## Fold, attention, first impressions
- NN/g, The Fold Manifesto: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/page-fold-manifesto/
  Fold still exists on every device. Users treat content above vs below the fold very differently. They scroll when the first screen is promising. A full-bleed first screen with no peek of what follows is a false floor.
- NN/g, Scrolling and Attention: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/
  Most viewing time is in the first two screenfuls. Closer to the top gets more attention. Used for "screens to first CTA / first product visual" and for not stuffing the hero.
- NN/g, 5-Second Usability Test: https://www.nngroup.com/videos/5-second-usability-test/
- NN/g, Testing Visual Design (5-second method): https://www.nngroup.com/articles/testing-visual-design/
  A timed first-impression check. Martin pairs it with "what is this, for whom, what to do" as a self-review, not as a lab study.

## Copy and above-the-fold craft
- Julian Shapiro, Startup Handbook: Landing Page Copywriting: https://www.julian.com/guide/startup/landing-pages
  Headline must name what you sell (slogan headers fail). Subhead 1-2 sentences. CTA continues the headline claim. Long pages are fine if the hero is tight. Get a stranger to review for clarity and brevity.
- Julian Shapiro / Demand Curve, Above the Fold: https://www.demandcurve.com/playbooks/above-the-fold
  In seconds visitors ask what you do and whether they are a fit. Specificity over corporate speak. Do not bloat the header with every objection.
- Existing copy skill (writes markdown, does not edit source or measure height): https://github.com/rampstackco/claude-skills/blob/HEAD/skills/landing-page-copy/SKILL.md
  Kept only: one primary CTA, headline / subhead / CTA as a trio, mismatched headline and CTA is a fail, no walls of text. Not adopted: their 7-section markdown template, or writing a new page instead of editing this one.

## Positioning
- April Dunford, A Quickstart Guide to Positioning: https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning
  Start from the alternative the customer would use if this product did not exist. Unique attributes, then value, then who cares, then the category that makes that value obvious. On the page: make the alternative and the difference obvious using only facts in the project. Never invent competitors, savings or a category.

## Scannable writing and disclosure
- NN/g, concise/scannable/objective: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/concise-scannable-and-objective-how-to-write-for-the-web/
- NN/g, how users read: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/
- NN/g, F-shaped pattern: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content-discovered/
- NN/g, Accordions on Desktop: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/accordions-on-desktop/
  Collapse secondary content only. Descriptive headings. Do not hide the offer, price or primary action.
- Baymard, trigger indicators for hidden UI: https://baymard.com/research-articles/trigger-indicators
  A collapsed block needs a trigger that looks expandable.
- Baymard, homepage and navigation: https://baymard.com/research/homepage-and-category-usability
  First screen should answer what this site is and what you can do. On ecommerce, a striking hero must not eclipse search and categories.
